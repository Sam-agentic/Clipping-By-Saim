🔥 Clipping by Saim

Paste a YouTube link. The app downloads the video, finds the strongest moments with AI and cuts them into ready-to-post clips. Working files are deleted automatically after export, so nothing piles up on disk.

Caption templates Click one — it applies to every clip, and each clip can still be changed on its own card.

Templates marked with a coloured word light up each word as it is spoken. The one-word templates put a single word on screen at a time. If a template looks plainer than its preview, this computer does not have that font and Windows substituted another one.

Seeing two sets of captions on a finished clip? The lower one is ours; the other is already burned into the downloaded video (a lot of highlight and podcast uploads ship that way). Pick No captions above — or on any single clip below — and only the original text remains. The at the top templates are the other way out: they put our text where the burned-in text is not.

🌐 Caption language

🔗 Platform presets

Leave that unticked and every template keeps the colours it was designed with. The headline is separate from the captions — it sits at the very top and disappears after the seconds you set.

No logo chosen

The logo goes in the top-right corner of every clip. A PNG with a see-through background looks best — a JPG brings its white box with it.

No folder chosen yet

Leave that off unless you plan to upload captions separately. The captions are already burned into the video, so the .srt is only an extra text file — those are the blank-looking files you saw next to the clips.

If no folder is chosen yet, the folder picker opens first — nothing is written or deleted until you pick one. Clips are copied into your folder, then the temporary project on this computer is deleted automatically.
